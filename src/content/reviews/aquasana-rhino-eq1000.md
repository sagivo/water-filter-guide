The Aquasana Rhino EQ-1000 pairs a carbon + KDF dual-tank system with a 0.35-micron post-filter, and its standout credential is system-level certification: per the maker's spec sheet, it's NSF/ANSI 42 certified with 97% chlorine reduction, with the tank WQA certified to NSF/ANSI 61. Flow is rated 7 GPM (14.6 peak) with 1,000,000 gallons of media capacity, and the price swings between ~$999 and ~$1,998 depending on bundle. **The one-line verdict:** the best certified chlorine-reduction pick — buy it when you want independent proof your chlorine is actually gone.

## Who the Aquasana Rhino EQ-1000 is for (and who should skip it)

Buy it if chlorine taste or odor is your main complaint and you want certified — not claimed — reduction. The dual-tank design suits mid-size homes, and the 0.35-micron post-filter adds a physical filtration stage most carbon systems skip.

Skip it if you're chasing PFAS or lead — this system's certifications don't cover them. Skip it too if frequent filter swaps annoy you: pre/post filters every 2–6 months is more maintenance than a [SpringWell CF1](/filters/springwell-cf1/). Budget shoppers should look at the [iSpring WGB32B](/filters/ispring-wgb32b/) instead.

## Filtration performance

Per the maker's spec sheet: carbon + KDF media in a dual-tank layout, finished with a 0.35-micron post-filter. The certification profile is the selling point — NSF/ANSI 42 certified as a system for 97% chlorine reduction, with the tank WQA certified to NSF/ANSI 61. That "system certified" wording matters: the finished unit's chlorine performance was tested, unlike component-level certs on the [SpringWell CF1](/filters/springwell-cf1/) or maker-only claims on budget systems. It's not a PFAS or lead system, though — see our [best whole house filters roundup](/posts/best-whole-house-water-filters-2026/) for how the certifications map to contaminants.

## Flow rate and pressure

Rated 7 GPM service with 14.6 GPM peak, the Rhino sits between the CF1's 9 and the HealthShield's 6. It suits typical 2–4 bath homes; if you're regularly pushing peak draws, verify with the [flow sizer](/tools/flow-sizer/).

## Installation and maintenance

Main-line install, and as a dual-tank plus post-filter setup it's a bit more involved than a single tank — most owners go pro. Maintenance is the recurring consideration: pre- and post-filters swap every 2–6 months at about $120 per year, while the tank media runs to 1,000,000 gallons. Aquasana backs it with a 10-year warranty.

## Cost of ownership

Pricing swings: ~$999–$1,998 depending on bundle (UV/conditioner options drive the spread). Add roughly $120 per year for pre/post filters, so five-year ownership lands somewhere between ~$1,599 and ~$2,598 before installation.
