The SpringWell CF1 is a whole house carbon + KDF filtration system built around a catalytic coconut-shell carbon tank, KDF media, and a 5-micron pre-filter — the standard recipe for city water that tastes or smells of chlorine. Per the maker's spec sheet, it's rated at 9 GPM service flow with a 1,000,000-gallon media capacity, and the components are certified to NSF/ANSI 42 and 53 (component-level, not whole-system). **The one-line verdict:** a strong, low-upkeep pick for most city-water homes in the 1–4 bathroom range.

## Who the SpringWell CF1 is for (and who should skip it)

Buy it if you're on municipal water and your main complaint is chlorine taste or odor, and your household is in the 1–4 bath range that a 9 GPM system comfortably covers. It's also a good fit if you like set-and-forget maintenance — media rated to a million gallons means you're not swapping cartridges every few months.

Skip it if you need whole-system certified contaminant reduction (PFAS, lead, or cysts) — component certifications don't carry the same weight. Skip it too if your home is larger than its 9 GPM rating comfortably serves; the [bigger sibling CF4](/filters/springwell-cf4/) steps up to 12 GPM. Well-water owners dealing with iron or sulfur should look at the [APEC CB3](/filters/apec-cb3/) instead.

## Filtration performance

The CF1 combines catalytic coconut-shell activated carbon with KDF media, fronted by a 5-micron pre-filter that catches sediment before it reaches the tank. Per the maker's spec sheet, this combination targets chlorine, chloramine, and the tastes and odors associated with municipal treatment.

On certification, precision matters: SpringWell says the CF1's components are certified to NSF/ANSI 42 and 53 — not the whole system. That's better than maker-claims-only, but not the same as a WQA-certified system like the [Aquasana HealthShield](/filters/aquasana-healthshield/), which is certified to NSF/ANSI 53 for PFAS and VOCs. If your concerns run to forever chemicals, read our [PFAS filter guide](/posts/pfas-forever-chemicals-filter-guide/) before deciding.

## Flow rate and pressure

The CF1 is rated for 9 GPM service flow, which suits most homes with 1–4 bathrooms running typical fixtures. Because whole-house pressure depends on your home's plumbing and incoming pressure as much as the filter, check our [flow sizer](/tools/flow-sizer/) if you're running multiple showers, irrigation, or other high-draw uses at once.

## Installation and maintenance

The CF1 installs on your main water line after the shutoff valve. Many owners handle it as a DIY project since it's a single tank with a pre-filter housing, but cutting into the main line is plumbing work — budget for a professional if you're not comfortable with it.

Maintenance is the CF1's quiet strength: the tank media is rated for up to 1,000,000 gallons, so there's no cartridge swap every few months. You'll occasionally change the 5-micron pre-filter sediment cartridge — a quick housing job. SpringWell backs the tank and valves with a lifetime warranty and a 6-month satisfaction guarantee.

## Cost of ownership

The system itself runs about ~$1,144–$1,160, and upkeep is estimated at roughly $35 per year for the sediment pre-filter. Over five years, that puts total ownership near ~$1,319–$1,335 plus any installation cost — one of the lowest long-run costs in its class, thanks to the long-lived media.
