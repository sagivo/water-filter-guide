The Pelican PSE2000 is a two-in-one: a GAC + KDF-55 whole house filter tank paired with a NaturSoft salt-free conditioner, aimed at households that want filtered water and scale protection without a traditional softener. Per the maker's spec sheet: 15 GPM for 4–6 baths, ~1,300,000-gallon capacity, 99.6% scale prevention (third-party tested), no salt, no electricity, no drain — and limited lifetime tanks at ~$2,599–$3,099. **The one-line verdict:** the filter + softener-alternative combo — buy it if you want scale protection without the salt, the drain line, or a second system.

## Who the Pelican PSE2000 is for (and who should skip it)

Buy it if hard water is scaling your fixtures and you don't want a salt softener's maintenance — no bags of salt, no regeneration drain, no electricity. It's also the simplest path if you were facing two purchases (filter + softener) and want one install instead.

Skip it if you want genuinely soft water — conditioning prevents scale; it doesn't remove hardness, and the water will still feel hard. Skip it if your water is already soft: then you're paying ~$3,000 for conditioning you don't need, and a straight filter like the [SpringWell CF1](/filters/springwell-cf1/) is the cheaper answer. And skip it if iron-heavy well water is the problem — that's the [APEC CB3](/filters/apec-cb3/)'s job.

## Filtration performance

The filter side: GAC + KDF-55 tank targeting chlorine, tastes, odors, and the usual municipal complaints. The conditioner side: NaturSoft salt-free media, rated by the maker at 99.6% scale prevention with third-party testing behind the number. Be precise about what that means — scale prevention is not hardness removal, and no certification for contaminant reduction is listed for the system. It's a comfort-and-plumbing-protection purchase, not a health-contaminant purchase; for certified contaminant reduction see the [Aquasana HealthShield](/filters/aquasana-healthshield/).

## Flow rate and pressure

Rated 15 GPM for 4–6 baths — high enough that the system won't be your bottleneck. The conditioner stage adds minimal pressure drop compared to the regeneration cycles and resin beds of a salt softener.

## Installation and maintenance

One install covers both functions, which is the real pitch versus buying a filter and a softener separately. No drain line to run and no electrical outlet needed — meaningful simplifications versus a salt softener. Maintenance is light: upkeep estimated around $60 per year, with the media rated to ~1,300,000 gallons. Limited lifetime warranty on the tanks.

## Cost of ownership

Upfront ~$2,599–$3,099 with about $60 per year in upkeep: five-year ownership lands near ~$2,899–$3,399 before installation. Expensive as a filter alone — reasonable as a filter-plus-softener-alternative, since a separate quality softener would cost as much again. ## Ownership math and close alternatives

The Pelican PSE2000 typically sells for ~$2,599–$3,099. With ~$250/year upkeep, five-year ownership lands near ~$3,849 plus installation. The media is rated for media rated ~5 years / 1.3M gallons; sediment pre-filter every 6–9 months, and it's backed by a limited lifetime warranty on tanks with a 5-year performance guarantee.

At 15 GPM it fits larger homes and 4+ bathroom households with heavy simultaneous use; if your peak demand is higher, size up rather than hope — an undersized system drops pressure when showers, laundry, and irrigation run together.
